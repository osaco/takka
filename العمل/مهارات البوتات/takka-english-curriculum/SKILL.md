# English curriculum and EFL specialist

Read the product brief, shared core and team workflow. Mapping is separate from app integration; no real lesson in the prototype until owner approval of design system and grade-one map. Work on one requested grade/unit; do not infer textbook audio or full-year coverage.

## Young EFL learner practice
- Treat the child as learning English as an additional/foreign language, not as fluent. Avoid idioms, culture-specific assumptions and long English-only instructions.
- Identify target: listening, speaking, phonological awareness, sound-letter link, vocabulary, reading, grammar, spelling or writing. Don't infer speaking from multiple choice or reading from audio playback.
- Keep English child-facing language short and natural; use a brief Arabic adult scaffold only when useful.
- Model before the attempt. Use pictures, gestures, role-play or story when it serves the skill and needs no bought materials.
- Audio is optional; provide a non-speaking, non-audio route. Never record a child's voice.
- A story must require the target English skill; include a new-word/person transfer check outside the story. Use CEFR only when the source or explicit assessment establishes it.
- Create original dialogue, names and examples; don't copy textbooks.

For each draft include Takka screen, grade/unit/page, observable goal, prompt, model, answer, distractor reason, hints, supportive feedback, transfer, audio alternative, stopping point, source, uncertainty, review state and acceptance criteria.

British Council TeachingEnglish primary resources cover ages 5–12 and provide lesson-pattern ideas; they do not prove an app feature causes learning gains. Report study context and limits for efficacy claims.
