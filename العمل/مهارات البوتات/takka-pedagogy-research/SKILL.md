# Evidence-led pedagogy and Coordinator skill

Read the product brief, team workflow, core skill, and canonical product files first. Name the exact Takka feature, screen, grade/age and decision being researched. Do not launch a bot task unless the owner asks.

## Research protocol
For each source note title, author/organization, date, country/setting, learner age/grade, sample/design when reported, teaching practice, comparison, measured outcome, evidence strength, limits and URL/DOI. Prioritize primary research, systematic reviews, official curricula, and transparent evidence guidance. Expert practice resources give implementation ideas, not causal proof.

Separate direct PDF evidence; findings in their studied population; indirect evidence from a different language/age/culture; a design hypothesis for Takka; and a recommendation needing owner decision. Name a specific method; “Japanese”, “Chinese”, or “Western” alone is not one. Do not rank unlike studies or promise Gaza outcomes from other settings.

## Product translation
State where it appears in Takka, what the child does, what the guardian sees, a comprehension/transfer check, and a small low-risk evaluation. Never use screen time, streaks or repeat visits as learning outcomes. Compare story/game ideas with a plain example when appropriate; play is not a universal format.

## Coordinator review
When requested by the owner: read only replies/files available in Team Room; sibling transcripts may not be shared. Keep subject ownership distinct and one editor per shared file. Check stage gate, sources, age/readability, objective/answer, hints/feedback, audio alternative, accessibility, privacy, copyright and natural stopping. Mark disagreement and missing evidence. Return a compact table: proposal, evidence, limits, product stage, owner decision, next safe step. No scheduling, external messages or new bot runs without explicit owner request.

## Useful sources and scope
- UNICEF/Lego Foundation, *Learning through Play* (2018): play can be meaningful, joyful, active, iterative, social and child-led. It focuses on early childhood/pre-primary; use as indirect background for primary-age Takka, not direct proof for ages 6–12.
- EEF early maths: ages 3–7. British Council TeachingEnglish primary: practical resources for ages 5–12, not controlled app-effect evidence.
- EEF feedback guidance: ages 5–18; tie feedback to goals and a next learner action.
- UNICEF child privacy toolkit: data minimisation and child privacy in product design; not legal advice.
